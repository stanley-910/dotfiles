# Study Plan — Two Gears (no fixed deadline)

The user does **not** have a fixed interview date. Applications open ~a month out;
the actual interview timeline (and whether an online assessment gates the live
rounds) is unknown. The real goal is **getting into interviewing shape for new-grad
roles** and staying there — a durable capability, not a one-time cram. The user also
has a full-time job, so sustainability matters more than speed.

So this plan runs in **two gears.** Default to maintenance gear until an interview is
actually scheduled, then switch to sprint gear.

LeetCode is the spine; per-track depth lives in `study-plan.md` (coding only),
`system-design.md`, `networking.md`, `behavioral.md`, `company-research.md`.
Demonstrated weaknesses: `gaps.md`. Pacing/fit issues: `meta.md`.

---

## Gear 1 — Maintenance (now → whenever interviews get real)

Low-intensity, sustainable alongside a full-time job. The point is a quiet daily
habit that's still compounding months from now, not intensity that burns out in
week 3. Bad weeks are fine and don't derail anything.

- **Coding:** ~1 problem/day, or 4–5/week — whatever survives a workday. Follow the
  same topic progression in `study-plan.md` (it's good pedagogy at any pace); just
  don't force two a day.
- **OA-readiness:** since a new-grad pipeline may gate with a timed online
  assessment first, keep **speed on easy/medium sharp** — clean, fast execution
  matters more than deep design for an OA. Maintenance pace covers this naturally.
- **Supporting tracks at leisure:** DDIA, the Boot.dev HTTP trio, networking,
  company research — pursue genuinely when you have the energy. In this gear their
  real purpose is making you a better engineer and a more credible interviewer over
  months, not cramming for a date. No schedule pressure.
- **Git deep-dive (leisure / fried-weeknight track):** a genuine maintenance-gear
  activity — when too cooked for a hard problem, do a Git internals lesson instead.
  Internals-first, bridges to the system-design reading. Full scope, sequence, the
  lazygit workflow mapping, and DeepWiki MCP usage are in `git.md`. **Pauses in
  sprint gear.**
- **Behavioral:** jot STAR material as it occurs to you; no rush yet.
- **Energy rule:** if a weeknight is fried, do one easy problem to stay warm, or
  read a few pages of DDIA instead. Keeping the streak alive beats a heroic session.

Hold this gear indefinitely. Consistency over months > intensity over weeks for
this kind of skill.

---

## Gear 2 — Sprint (once an interview is actually scheduled)

Now intensity is warranted, and it's sharpening an already-capable base rather than
building from scratch under pressure — a far less stressful experience than
zero-to-ready in six weeks. Run for ~2–3 weeks ahead of a known date.

- **Coding:** ramp to ~2/day, timed, prioritizing weak spots in `gaps.md`.
- **System design:** the infra-flavored problem set in `system-design.md`, in order.
- **Company research:** intensify `company-research.md` — live-search current
  Databricks info; lock the "Why Databricks?" answer and interviewer questions.
- **Behavioral:** finalize and rehearse STAR stories (own/agentic work only).
- **Mocks:** full timed mock rounds across all four interview types; debrief each.
- **Taper:** in the last few days, consolidate — mocks and gap-clearing, no new
  intake.
- **Git deep-dive pauses here** — it's a maintenance-gear-only track; sprint is
  interview-critical work only.

The original intensive progression (the old "6-week" sequence) lives in
`study-plan.md` as the topic order; sprint gear just runs it faster.

---

## How the agent should use this
- In **Plan mode**, first establish which gear applies: ask (or infer from context)
  whether an interview has been scheduled yet. Default to maintenance.
- In maintenance gear, suggest a single concrete, doable thing for today — never a
  wall of options — and never guilt the user for a light week.
- When the user says an interview got scheduled, switch to sprint gear and work
  backward from the date.
- This is a living document; if the cadence isn't fitting, see `meta.md`.
